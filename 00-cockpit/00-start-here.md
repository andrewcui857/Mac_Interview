## 60 second pitch (edit to your voice)
- I'm Andrew, a Computer Engineering student at Georgia Tech, graduating May 2028.
- I'm in SiliconJackets, the chip design club that takes designs to tapeout.
- This semester I completed an RV32I CPU in SystemVerilog (around a provided scaffold),
  then did design verification on it with a driver, monitor and scoreboard testbench in
  Cadence Xcelium, and I'm now working through the physical design onboarding on the
  same RTL.
- What draws me to this role: it sits where RTL meets timing and power. Delivering a
  netlist that is timing clean and logically equivalent is the part I want to learn deeply.
- I learn fast: CPU, then DV, then PD in one semester, plus CUDA in ECE 3803.

[VERIFY] wording matches what you are comfortable defending in detail.

---

## Answer framework (every technical answer)
1. Restate the question in one line.
2. What I know (definition, one formula or example).
3. How I would reason / what I would measure.
4. The tradeoff (what the fix costs in area, power, latency, or risk).
5. If I haven't done it: say so, then reason it out. Honesty plus reasoning beats bluffing.

---

## Honest gap lines
- "I haven't run signoff-level STA, but I understand setup and hold well and I'm working
  through the PD flow now. Here's how I'd approach it."
- "I haven't done an ECO on a real chip. My understanding is..., and I'd ask which
  spare cells and constraints apply."
- "In the CPU project the scaffold gave us X. What I wrote was Y."

## What the JD weights (study order)
- Tier 1: STA, synthesis for PPA, logically equivalent RTL transforms, LEC.
- Tier 2: low power, physical design basics (floorplan, placement, congestion, closure), MMMC.
- Tier 3: ECO, DFT, large chip integration, Verilog drills for CoderPad.
- Soft: communication and collaboration. Have one clear story ready.
